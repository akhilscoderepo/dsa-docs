## Unlocked Combinations

One combination is released in this chapter with its own lesson and ladder, and one is named as deferred together with the chapter that supplies what it lacks.

### Teach Now

Breadth-first search read together with state modeling is the sixth lesson. The search contributes the layer-by-layer order that makes a first discovery shortest, and the modeling contributes the decision about what counts as one distinct vertex and what the visited mark must remember. When several starts and a changing state are mixed, the mistakes move from the loop to the encoding, which is why the ladder re-asks familiar problems with new contracts: a spread that is stopped by walls, nearest zeros on a board whose edges wrap, a diagonal board that reports the cells lying on shortest routes, and a word ladder that counts shortest ladders instead of measuring one. The false friend is a mark kept on a visible location while a hidden fact changes the options.

### Deferred

Searches whose steps have different costs need a frontier ordered by cost rather than by round, either with a priority queue or with a double-ended queue for zero-one weights. Both rest on weighted relaxation and not on first discovery, so they are owned by Chapter 24 and no problem of that kind is assigned here. The heap itself comes from Chapter 17, and a graph combined with a heap is released only once the weighted chapter is reached.

### Already Covered

The ordinary single-source search, the mark on entry, grids as implicit graphs and the unit-cost shortest path come from Chapter 21. The queue behind the frontier was built in Chapter 11, and hashing of states into sets and maps comes from Chapter 04.
