## Unlocked Combinations

Every ingredient of one combination in this chapter is now in place, so that combination has its own lesson and its own four-rung ladder. One more is named as deferred, with the chapter that owns its missing piece.

### Teach Now

Sorting with intervals is the seventh lesson. Sorting contributes an order in which a single carried number can stand for everything already seen, and the interval rules contribute the meaning of overlap and of a better choice. The lesson uses one pattern to answer four questions: blocks with a count of the sessions they swallow, a position and an absorbed count for an insertion into a sorted list, the indices of a kept set under a written tie rule, and the coordinates of a shortest list of announcements. The ladder moves from a counted union through an ordered insertion and a selection that names original positions to a list of chosen coordinates. The false friend is the belief that sorting alone fixes the answer, when the key and the update are what decide it.

### Deferred

Allocation problems such as counting the rooms a set of meetings needs call for a heap that keeps the earliest end among the rooms in use. Chapter 17 teaches that heap invariant and owns the whole combination ladder, so this chapter assigns no problem that needs a priority queue and treats a sweep with a plain counter as the limit of what it can answer.

### Already Covered

Sorting with a comparator, and the rule that a comparator must never subtract extreme values, were taught in Chapter 05. The cursor in each of two ordered sequences came from Chapter 08, and the difference array that counts coverage by adding at a start and subtracting after an end was taught in Chapter 07. The sweep here reuses that counting idea, with sorted entries in place of an array indexed by coordinate.
