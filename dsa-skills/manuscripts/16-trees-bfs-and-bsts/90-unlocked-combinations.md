## Unlocked Combinations

Two combinations have every ingredient in place and each receives a lesson and a practice ladder of its own. One further idea is recorded as deferred, with the chapter that supplies the missing piece.

### Teach Now

The tree together with a breadth-first queue is the tenth lesson. The tree supplies the children of each node and the queue supplies discovery in order of depth, while a size captured before each wave marks where the wave ends. The ladder changes what each wave leaves behind: a count and a sum, a reversal applied to every k-th wave, both ends of every wave with a hidden count, and finally a tree whose nodes have any number of children. The false friend is reversing the order of enqueueing to reverse a wave, which changes who is discovered next.

The search tree together with an inherited interval is the eleventh lesson. The ordering promise decides where to walk, and the interval carried from the top states which keys are still possible. The ladder reads the interval in four ways: as the neighbours of a missing key, as the test that finds the first violation of the numbering, as a filter for a rank inside a band, and as the limit on what may replace a removed key. The false friend is trusting the check of a parent against its children.

### Deferred

A tree stored as an array in which the children of position i sit at 2i+1 and 2i+2 is the layout of a heap, and walking it needs a priority rule that this chapter does not teach. The tree part is ready, but the heap order, sift operations and priority queues belong to Chapter 17. No heap traversal problem is assigned here. Walking a general graph by waves needs a visited set, which belongs to Chapters 21 and 22.

### Already Covered

The queue discipline came from Chapter 11, and the stack walk used by the range and iterator lessons relies on its last-in, first-out order. Recursive return values for trees were taught in Chapter 15, and the lowest common ancestor lesson builds on that return habit. The halving argument behind a search tree was introduced in Chapter 06, and the idea of keeping an invariant across steps was practised in Chapter 14.
