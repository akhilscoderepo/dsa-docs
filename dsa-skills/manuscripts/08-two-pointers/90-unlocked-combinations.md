## Unlocked Combinations

Three combinations are released by this chapter, because each of their ingredients has already been taught, and every one has its own lesson with a four-rung ladder. The remaining combinations are named with an owner, and none of their problems are assigned here.

### Teach Now

Sorting with two pointers is the eighth lesson. Chapter 05 supplied the order, and the first, fifth and sixth lessons of this chapter supplied the pair scan, the skip rule and the fixed leading values. Together they handle pair, triple and quadruple searches in quadratic or cubic time instead of trying every combination. The false friend is running the pointers on data that was never sorted.

Strings with two pointers is the ninth lesson. The string chapter supplied indexed access, normalization and mutable character arrays, and this chapter supplied symmetric and same-direction movement. The ladder runs from a cleaned palindrome check through in-place reversal and one allowed deletion to a subsequence test whose pointers move at different rates. The false friend is applying palindrome movement to a task whose pointers both travel left to right.

Index storage with fast and slow pointers is the tenth lesson. The array chapter supplied the idea that a value can name a position, and the seventh lesson of this chapter supplied the walk that finds where a cycle begins. The false friend is a mutating technique that would break a read-only promise.

### Deferred

Sliding window combines two same-direction pointers with a maintained summary of the range between them, and it belongs to Chapter 09. Linked-list cycle and midpoint problems reuse the fast and slow walk over node references, and they belong to Chapter 14. Greedy interval selection that sorts and sweeps belongs to Chapter 20. No problems from those chapters are assigned here.

### Already Covered

Sorting with a comparator was taught in Chapter 05 and is only used here. Hash-map pair search was taught in Chapter 04, and the sorted pair scan of the first lesson is the alternative that needs no extra memory. The array-as-storage idea is Chapter 01 material and returns in the tenth lesson with its bounds contract restated.
