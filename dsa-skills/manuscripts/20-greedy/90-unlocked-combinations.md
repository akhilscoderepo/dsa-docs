## Unlocked Combinations

Four combinations are released in this chapter, each with a lesson and a ladder of its own, and one more is recorded as deferred together with the chapters that supply what it lacks.

### Teach Now

Greedy and ordering is the sixth lesson. A sort key puts the dominant item first, and an exchange or stays-ahead argument gives the right to commit it. The ladder runs from assignment, through interval removal with a reported boundary and arrow placement over closed spans, to cutting a string at the earliest legal position. Its false friend is sorting by the wrong key, such as start time when the task is selection.

Greedy and heap is the seventh lesson. The heap of Chapter 17 holds the candidates, and the proof says which extreme to retract or to take. The ladder covers ramps and winches, fuel stops, a schedule of courses with its total, and a pool of affordable projects. Its false friend is a heap used without the proof of which end is safe.

Greedy and monotonic stack is the eighth lesson. The stack of Chapter 12 holds the best prefix, and the proof permits a pop only under a budget, a promise of return, or a length requirement. The ladder moves from digit removal to distinct letters, to distinct characters under a custom rank order, to subsequences of fixed length.

Greedy and two pointers is the ninth lesson. Sorted order from the earlier chapters makes each pointer move a discard, and the exchange argument shows that the discard loses nothing. The ladder pairs cookies within a tolerance, fills boats from both ends, handles the exact-fit boundary, and plays a token game that spends at one end and sells at the other.

### Deferred

Some problems look greedy and are not. Coin change with denominations that lack a safe largest-first order, and weighted interval scheduling, need a comparison of reusable future states, which is the subject of the dynamic programming chapters. Chapters 26 to 29 own that framework and all of its problems, so none is assigned here. The false friend in the first lesson, the largest coin first, is the pointer to that handover.

### Already Covered

Heap-based interval problems such as counting rooms are not repeated, since the heap and interval ideas meet in Chapter 17, and sorting-based interval merging was taught in Chapter 10, where the same problems appeared with different contracts. The mechanics of pointer motion are from Chapter 08, those of the monotonic stack are from Chapter 12, and the comparator rules and the sorting of index arrays are from Chapter 05.
