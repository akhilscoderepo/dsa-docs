## Unlocked Combinations

A sorted scan that never changes its mind passes every sample and fails one hidden input. This chapter releases four pairings in full and names one that waits for a later chapter.

### Pairings Taught In This Chapter

Four pairings are released, and each has its own lesson and its own ladder.

Ordering and the exchange argument form the lesson Sort Then Commit Greedily. The order supplies the front item, and the argument supplies the reason to commit to it. The lesson adds one idea. The sort key is the number that the exchange step compares, and for a string cut into parts that number is the last position of each letter. Its exercises cover the satisfied children of a cookie match, a gap between kept intervals, half-open balloons and the lengths of the parts of a stream.

A heap and the greedy rule form the lesson Undo The Worst Choice With A Heap. The rule names which earlier choice to withdraw, and the heap finds it in logarithmic time. The lesson adds one idea. A choice is made tentatively and withdrawn only when a resource runs out. Its exercises cover climbing with ladders and bricks, refueling stops, a course schedule that also reports its total time, and projects unlocked by capital.

A monotonic stack and the greedy rule form the lesson Drop Earlier Items With A Stack. The stack supplies the nearest worse item, and the rule limits the pops with a budget. The lesson adds one idea. A pop is safe only while deletions remain, or while enough items remain to reach the required length. Its exercises cover deleting digits, keeping one copy of each letter, reporting positions and a fixed-length subsequence.

Two pointers and the greedy rule form the lesson Pair Items From Both Ends. The sorted order makes the lightest and the heaviest items the ends, and the rule decides which pointer may move. The lesson adds one idea. The heaviest item is settled first, because the lightest item is its best partner. Its exercises cover cookies with an upper bound, boats, an exact-capacity count and a token game.

### One Pairing Waits For A Later Chapter

Some problems look greedy but compare reusable future states, and a local rule fails on them. Telling them apart needs the state framework of Chapters 26 to 29, and those chapters own the problems of that pairing.
