## Unlocked Combinations

This chapter releases one combination, because every ingredient has been taught, and gives it a lesson of its own with a separate four-rung ladder. A second combination is named as deferred, together with the chapter that supplies what it lacks.

### Teach Now

The ordered stack together with contribution counting is the eighth lesson. The stack supplies the nearest beating position on each side of every element in a single pass. Counting turns those two positions into widths for rectangles or into products of free starts and free ends for totals over all subarrays. The lesson closes with a sum of spreads, which needs two ownership passes with the same tie convention. The ladder runs from answers keyed by position, through waits folded into aggregates and a rectangle reported with its span, to a total over two extremes. The false friend is the rectangle width used as if it were a count of subarrays.

### Deferred

Problems in which an earlier choice is dropped because a limited budget of removals makes it provably useless, such as shrinking a number by deleting digits, use a stack whose order comes from an exchange argument. The stack alone cannot supply that proof, and the argument belongs to Chapter 20 on greedy methods. No problem of that kind is assigned in this chapter. Dynamic programming states that borrow a stack are also left for later chapters.

### Already Covered

The plain stack, with its end chosen once and kept, was taught in Chapter 11, and the lessons here use it without re-teaching its methods. Counts kept per key and maps from values to answers come from Chapter 04, and the prefix sum idea of Chapter 07 is used only as a different way to see a total. Pointer movement with an invariant, from Chapter 08, is the model for the argument that each position is handled a constant number of times.
