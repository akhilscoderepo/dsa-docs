## Unlocked Combinations

A log with one hundred thousand readings makes a loop over every range impractical, and a stack that finds boundaries plus a count removes the loop. This chapter releases one pairing that its prerequisites allow, and it names one pairing that waits for a later proof.

### Pairing Taught In This Chapter

The stack from Chapter 11 and the boundary counting of this chapter form the lesson Count Subarrays From Stack Boundaries. The stack names the neighbors of each leaving index, and the counting turns the two neighbors into a number of ranges or into a rectangle width. The lesson adds one idea: the product of two distances is final at the moment an index leaves, so sums over all ranges need no extra arrays. Its exercises cover the pairs produced by each removal, the longest wait among all days, the largest rectangle with its left index, and a sum of maximums with negative values.

### One Pairing Waits For A Later Proof

A stack can also remove digits from a number to make the result as small as possible. That removal needs a proof that an earlier digit never helps once a later digit is smaller, and the proof belongs to the greedy chapter. Chapter 20 owns this pairing, and this chapter assigns none of its problems.

### What Later Chapters Reuse

Three ideas carry forward.

- **The owned count** returns whenever a total is split across the items that own its parts.
- **The leaving moment** returns whenever an answer is final only when an item leaves a structure.
- **The tie rule** returns whenever equal items could claim the same part twice.
