## Unlocked Combinations

This chapter releases one combination, which has a lesson and a ladder of its own, and it records one more as deferred together with the chapter that supplies what is missing.

### Teach Now

A trie read together with a string is the sixth lesson. The string supplies the next letter and its own end, and the trie supplies a node that stands for every stored word sharing the letters already read. What needs care is the rule that decides when the reading stops, because the first flag, the last flag and a chain of flags give three different problems. The ladder begins with exact and beginning questions that also count distinct words, continues with a blank-matching search that reports how many nodes it entered, then replaces words by their shortest root, and ends with the longest word built one letter at a time. The false friend is the repeated scan of the whole list for every word of the text.

### Deferred

A trie can also drive a search over a board of letters, so that many target words are tested at once while one path is walked. The trie half is ready, but the walk needs a path that is chosen, explored and then undone, together with a record of which cells are in use, and that state of choose, explore and unchoose is taught in Chapter 19. That chapter owns the board problems, so none is assigned here.

### Already Covered

The memoization of word-break answers is not a combination of this chapter, and the lesson only names it: the state of a start index and the saving from remembering it belong to Chapter 26. The reading of a string by index comes from Chapter 03, the sets and maps that serve as the trie's rivals come from Chapter 04, and the argument that a high bit outweighs all lower bits echoes the place-value reasoning of the number lessons in Chapter 00.
