## Unlocked Combinations

Every ingredient of one combination has now been taught, so it receives its own lesson and its own four-rung ladder. A second combination is listed as deferred, together with the chapters that own what it still needs.

### Teach Now

The deque with the sliding window is the ninth lesson. The window contributes the edges that only move forward and the rule that expires old positions, and the deque contributes the value order that makes an extreme available at the front. The lesson shows one skeleton answering three questions: a sum of window maxima, a count of subarrays whose range stays within a tolerance, and the shortest run reaching a target when the start must not be too old. The ladder goes from a counted trace of both removals, through a changed total, to a count that needs both deques and a jumping left pointer, and ends with a prefix-sum deque under a length cap. The false friend is a plain window with running extremes, which cannot recover after the extreme expires.

### Deferred

Zero-one breadth-first search keeps a deque of graph states and places free moves ahead of paid moves. Its correctness depends on shortest-path reasoning and on a model of graph state that the curriculum has not reached. Chapter 21 introduces graph traversal and Chapter 24 owns shortest paths, and this chapter assigns no problem of that kind.

### Already Covered

The use of `ArrayDeque` as a plain stack was taught in Chapter 11, and the lessons here rely on it without repeating the method list. The window with edges that move forward was taught in Chapter 09, and the sum of a run as a difference of cumulative values was taught in Chapter 07. The reasoning that each position enters and leaves a structure once, which bounds the total work, follows the stack argument of Chapter 12.
