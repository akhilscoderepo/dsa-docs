## Unlocked Combinations

Sorting opens one pairing that this chapter teaches in full, links one pairing to lessons that already exist, and leaves several pairings to chapters that own their prerequisites.

### Pairing Taught In This Chapter

Strings with maps and sorting is the lesson Group Strings By Sorted Letters. The string supplies the letters, the sort turns them into one standard sequence, and the map collects the words that share that sequence. The lesson closes with the swap-and-exchange rules, where the sorted letters alone are not the right name.

### Pairing Covered In Earlier Lessons

Arrays with sorting needs no separate lesson. The lessons Sweep A Sorted Array, Remove Duplicates After Sorting and Scan The Sorted Array each own one relation between a sorted array and a later decision.

### Pairings That Wait For Later Chapters

Sorting with two pointers belongs to Chapter 08. That chapter teaches how two indexes move safely over a sorted array and how a duplicate policy behaves while they move. Sorting with binary search belongs to Chapter 06, which teaches the yes-or-no question that a binary search needs. Sorting with intervals belongs to Chapter 10, where the rules for touching endpoints are stated. Sorting with greedy choice belongs to Chapter 20, which gives the proof that a locally best choice stays best. Sorting with selection of the k-th value, without a full sort, belongs to Chapter 32. No exercise in this chapter assigns a problem from those pairings.

### What Later Chapters Reuse

Three ideas carry forward.

- **The comparator with a stated tie rule** returns in the heap chapter and in every chapter that orders records.
- **The sweep with one summary** returns in the interval chapter, where the summary is the end of the last merged range.
- **The sorted letters as a name** returns in the string chapters that group words by a shared property.
