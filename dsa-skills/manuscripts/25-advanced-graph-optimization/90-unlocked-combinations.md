## Unlocked Combinations

A planner asks for the cheapest cable plan, the operations team asks which single links split the network, and a dispatcher asks for a tour that drives every link once. All three questions use the same map, and a shortest path search answers none of them correctly. This chapter releases one pairing, where a pairing is a lesson that joins earlier topics into one decision, and it leaves one pairing for later.

### Pairing Taught In This Chapter

The lesson Choose A Method For Each Graph Goal joins graph optimization with union-find and the heap. The heap and the sorted edge list come from earlier lessons, union-find supplies the group test, and the chapter methods supply low links, the distance table and the trail walk. The lesson adds one idea: the goal of the statement decides which fact the program must keep true, and a method that keeps a different fact gives a wrong or slow answer. Its four exercises cover a cable plan with a group target, bridges filtered by size, a city count with a strict limit, and an itinerary in dictionary order.

### Pairing That Waits For A Later Chapter

Flow and matching need a residual graph, which records how much capacity each edge still has, and an invariant about augmenting paths that grow a flow step by step. No lesson of this book teaches that invariant yet, so this chapter assigns no exercises for it. The owner is a future chapter on flow and matching, and until it exists the topic stays outside the active curriculum.
